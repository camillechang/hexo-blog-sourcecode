### Still Searching: Joy, Meaning, and the Next Goal

This year I've been "exploring the world"—not only stamps in a passport, but an inner map: what kind of life I still want, and who I still want to become. The strange part is, the more I move outward and the more people I talk to, the more often an ordinary afternoon stops me cold: **what are we really living for?** Sometimes it feels like boredom—not that there is nothing to do, but that after doing it, something inside is still empty.

#### A Conversation with Friends

Today I talked with a few women friends. Everyone had her own troubles in relationships—tension, hurts that are hard to name, wanting to be understood but afraid to say too much. Those who are single hope to meet the right person, and at the same time ask: **when there is no relationship, where does the joy in life come from?** We seemed to be discussing different things, but underneath ran the same thread: we are all looking for meaning, or at least something steady enough to hold an ordinary day.

#### What Makes Me Happy?

I am happy when I dance; when the song ends, the happiness ebbs away. I am happy when I exercise; after a shower I sit down, and it is ordinary again. I am happy during sex, and often satisfied afterward—but after a few days the satisfaction fades. **A lot of happiness is event-shaped**: it lives in the moment of doing something, not as a permanent state. That does not make it less real; it was never meant to stand guard over a whole life.

#### Goals and Emptiness

The days that felt most full were often days when **I had a goal and moved toward it every day**, even by a small step. I was not happy every day, but I rarely felt that life was pointless. Goals have a harsh side too: once you reach one, you need the next. When the goal is too far away, or not yet chosen, emptiness seeps back—and that line appears: tomorrow you might die; you never know whether it is tomorrow or an accident. Everything feels light, so light it feels hollow.

#### Still Practicing

"Exploring the world" this year taught me many things, but it did not hand me a single correct answer. Maybe meaning is not something you find once and save; maybe happiness is like that hour of dancing—**brief, and real.** I am still practicing: after pleasure fades, not calling it fake; after a goal is reached, allowing a gap before asking whether the next direction is something I want, or only a way to avoid emptiness. Today's conversation had no conclusion. We sat in the same fog. **I am not the only one still searching.**

#### Books, Two Countries, and Loneliness

Sometimes I wonder: are these thoughts because I **read too little** and **have seen too little of the world?** I have lived in and traveled to two countries. After that, I do not especially long to collect more destinations—I want to **read**. Books hold lives that already happened in reality; through them I borrow other people's courage, mistakes, and passions.

I envy people who seem to have real hobbies and full days, who do not feel lonely as often. I want that too—not to perform a "rich life" for Instagram, but so that when the high of dancing or exercise passes, something **stays**: a half-finished book, a class on Wednesday, a project that grows week by week.

I don't think the problem is simply "not enough countries." Geography widens the map; it does not always teach you how to **live with yourself on a quiet Tuesday.** Reading less might narrow my language for feelings, but reading more alone won't replace showing up—picking one or two interests and letting them compound.

**Two countries is already a life, not a failure of curiosity.** Preferring books over boarding passes is a valid way to explore. What I may still lack is not passport stamps but **rhythm**: small habits that return, people I see regularly, goals close enough to touch. That is what I am trying to build next—not to erase the big questions, but to give the empty afternoons something to lean on.

The road is long. I do not know if tomorrow will come; but today we talked, I danced, I loved, I thought—and for now, that is enough.

## 中文版

今年我一直在「探索世界」——不只是护照上的印章，更是心里的地图：想知道自己还想要什么样的生活，还想成为什么样的人。奇怪的是，越是往外走、越是和不同的人交谈，越容易在某个普通的下午突然愣住：**人活着，到底图什么？** 有时候会觉得很无聊：不是没事做，而是做完一件事，心里还是空的。

### 和几个女生的聊天

今天跟几个女生聊天。大家各有各的愁：关系里的拉扯、说不清的委屈、想要被理解又害怕说透。单身的，一边盼着遇见对的人，一边又问：**没有那段关系的时候，生活的乐趣要从哪里来？** 聊着聊着发现，底下是同一条线——**都在找生命的意义，或者说，找一点能撑住日常的东西。**

### 什么能让我开心？

跳舞的时候是开心的，舞曲结束，开心像退潮。运动的时候是开心的，冲完澡坐下，又与平常一样。性爱的时候是开心的，之后常常是满足的，可过几天，满足也会淡下去。我渐渐看清：**很多快乐是事件型的**——发生在当下的那一小时里，不是要替整个人生站岗；短暂，但可以是真的。

### 目标与空虚

那些最充实的日子，往往是**心里有一个目标，并且每天都在朝它挪**，哪怕只挪一小步。那时候不一定天天快乐，但很少觉得「活着没意思」。可目标一旦到达，就要再设下一个；目标太远或还没想好，空虚就又渗回来——脑子里飘着：反正明天也可能死，明天和意外不知道哪个先来。这样想的时候，不是想自杀，而是觉得**一切都很轻，轻到有点虚。**

### 我还在练习

「探索世界」今年教会我很多，却没给我标准答案。也许意义不是找到一次就存档；也许开心像跳舞那一小时——**短暂，但真实。** 我还在练习：乐趣消退后，不急着否定刚才的快乐；目标达成后，允许自己空一阵子，再问**下一个，是我想走的，还是只是为了不让自己空？** 今天没有结论，只是并排坐在同一片雾里。**原来不是我一个人一直在找。**

### 书、两个国家，和寂寞

我有时候会想：这些念头，是不是因为我**书读得太少**、**见过的世界也不够大？** 我其实已经在两个国家生活、旅行过了。在那之后，我好像并没有特别向往再去别的国家，反而更想**看书**——书里的许多事，在现实里早已发生过；我也可以通过别人的文字，借一点他们的勇气、弯路和热爱。

我也羡慕那些好像总有爱好、日子很充实、不那么寂寞的人。我也想那样——不是为了表演一种「精彩人生」，而是希望跳舞或运动的高潮过去之后，手里还有**下一页没读完的书、每周三的一堂课、一点点在变长的作品**。

我不觉得问题只是「国家去得不够多」。远行能拓宽地图，却不一定教会你**如何在平平无奇的周二和自己相处。** 读得少，也许少一些形容感受的词；但只多读书，也不能代替**真的去练一件事**——选一两样兴趣，让它慢慢叠起来。

**两个国家，已经是一种人生，不是对好奇心的不及格。** 比起集邮式旅行，更想用书去探索，这本身也没关系。我可能缺的与其说是护照印章，不如说是**节奏**：会回来的小习惯、常见面的人、够得着的目标。接下来想建的，是这些——不是为了消灭那些大问题，而是给空着的下午，一点可以靠一靠的东西。

路还长。明天会不会来，我不知道；但今天聊过天、跳过舞、爱过、想过、也写下这些——暂时就够了。
