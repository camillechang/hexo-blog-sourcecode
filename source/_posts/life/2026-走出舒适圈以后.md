在澳大利亚生活的前七年，我几乎没有出去旅行过。

我的生活慢慢变得封闭，也在一段很糟糕的关系里消耗了很长时间。日子一直在过，但回头看时，好像没有留下多少真正属于自己的记忆。

到了 2026 年，我终于开始走出舒适圈。

这一年还没有结束，但它好像已经比过去七八年加起来都更精彩。

## 为什么长大以后，时间过得越来越快

小时候，总觉得一天很长，一年更是遥远得看不到尽头。

长大以后，时间却像突然按下了快进键。一周接着一周，一年好像什么都没有发生就结束了。

后来我看到一种解释：并不只是时间变快了，而是成年后的我们经常重复同样的生活。去同一个地方上班，做熟悉的事情，回家以后继续相似的夜晚。大脑没有留下足够多的新记忆，回头看时，那段时间就被压缩成了模糊的一小块。

小的时候，很多事情都是第一次。第一次上学，第一次交朋友，第一次去陌生的地方。每一个“第一次”都会留下清晰的记忆，所以童年显得格外漫长。

也许 2026 年让我感觉如此丰富，正是因为这一年里有太多第一次。

我不能让时间停下来，但我可以让走过的时间留下更多痕迹。

## 先让身体动起来

我开始规律地去健身房，也尝试了普拉提和公园跑步。

以前的我可能会因为不熟悉、怕做不好，干脆不开始。现在我慢慢发现，很多事情不需要等到准备好了才去做。先出现，先尝试，身体和信心都会慢慢跟上来。

健身带给我的不只是外形变化。它让我感觉自己有能力照顾好身体，也有能力一点点改变生活。

## 去靠近舞台和人群

我去电视节目录制现场当过观众，也尝试做过竞赛选手。我去学画画，上了两个月的即兴表演课程，还开始练习主动和陌生人说话。

我也开始一个人去不同的脱口秀俱乐部，看别人站在台上讲自己的故事。

后来，我给自己报名了一个两个月的脱口秀课程。

我认识了一些新朋友，社交圈也一点点扩大。以前觉得只有那些天生外向、特别有趣的人才适合站上舞台，现在才明白，勇气并不是不紧张，而是紧张的时候仍然愿意往前走一步。

## 关于约会，我仍然没有答案

今年我也体验了线下快速约会、慢速约会和线上约会。

我曾经认真期待一段长期关系，最后却很失望。到现在，我甚至还没有完全想明白，自己究竟想寻找怎样的关系。

但这个过程至少让我看见：我很容易羡慕一个人身上拥有的自由、独立和与自己相处的能力，然后在喜欢对方时，为他加上一层滤镜。

与其崇拜别人拥有的生活，我也可以慢慢建立自己的生活。

## 我也可以成为很酷的人

我觉得骑摩托车的人很酷，所以自己报名学习了摩托车。

当我真的开始学习以后，那种遥不可及的崇拜感反而减少了。原来很多看起来很厉害的事情，并不是某些人的专属。

我也可以学会。

我也可以成为自己觉得很酷的人。

## 第一次独自去巴厘岛

今年，我终于一个人去了巴厘岛。

出发前，我本来还想着约会、认识帅哥。真正到了以后，我却尝试了很多完全不同的东西：跟陌生人去玩滑翔伞，一个人去酒吧，去健身房，坐摩托车，在海边发呆，也试着认识新朋友。

最后，我并没有真正交到多少朋友。

但我发现，旅行不一定要靠认识很多人来证明它成功。一个人回到房间、一个人吃饭、一个人决定下一站去哪里，也可以很自在。

我没有在巴厘岛找到一段关系，却发现自己更爱原来的生活，也更珍惜自己已经拥有的一切。

## 世界突然变大了

从巴厘岛回来以后，公司又给了我一次去美国学习的机会。

我突然感觉，世界真的很大，也很精彩。

以前的我好像一直站在同一个地方，以为人生只有眼前的工作、关系和烦恼。真正走出去以后才发现，生活还有健身房、舞台、画画、摩托车、旅行、陌生人和很多尚未抵达的地方。

## 见过世界以后，仍然保持柔软

旅行中，我也遇见过一些已经看过很多地方、经历过很多事情的人。

他们见过更大的世界以后，好像不再羡慕浪漫的爱情，也很少有什么事情能够让他们真正兴奋。也许当新鲜和刺激变成日常，人会越来越难被打动。

但我不希望自己变成那样。

我希望无论以后去过多少国家、见过多大的世界，依然保留一颗柔软的心。依然愿意爱人，愿意善良，会因为一次日落、一阵海风、一顿好吃的饭，或者陌生人的一点善意而开心。

见多识广不应该意味着冷漠。真正丰富的经历，也许不是让人觉得“不过如此”，而是让人更理解不同的生活，也更懂得珍惜。

我希望自己不断向外探索，却不会失去内心感受幸福的能力。

看过世界以后，依然对世界心存感激。

走出舒适圈以后，我的生活并没有突然变得完美。

我仍然会孤独，会在关系里受伤，会害怕，也会不知道下一步应该去哪里。

但现在的我知道，即使没有人陪我，我也可以出发。

生命不一定要用年份来衡量。有时候，认真生活一年，真的可以比重复过去七年留下更多记忆。

2026 年，我没有找到所有答案。

但我终于重新开始生活了。

## English Version

### 2026: The Year I Left My Comfort Zone and the World Became Bigger

During my first seven years in Australia, I hardly travelled at all.

My life gradually became smaller, and I spent a long time being drained by an unhealthy relationship. The days continued to pass, but when I looked back, very few memories felt truly mine.

In 2026, I finally began stepping outside my comfort zone.

The year is not over yet, but it already feels richer than the previous seven or eight years combined.

#### Why Time Feels Faster as We Grow Older

As a child, a single day felt long, and a year seemed almost endless.

As an adult, life appears to move in fast-forward. One week follows another, and an entire year can end before it feels as though anything happened.

One explanation stayed with me: time does not simply speed up. Adults often repeat the same routines—travelling to the same workplace, doing familiar tasks, and returning home to similar evenings. When the brain records fewer distinctive memories, that period becomes compressed when we look back on it.

Childhood is full of firsts: a first day at school, a first friendship, a first visit to an unfamiliar place. Each new experience creates a marker in memory, which may be why those years feel so long.

Perhaps 2026 has felt so rich because it has contained so many firsts.

I cannot stop time from moving, but I can give it more memories to leave behind.

#### I Started by Moving My Body

I began going to the gym consistently. I also tried Pilates and running in the park.

In the past, being unfamiliar with something—or being afraid of doing it badly—was enough to stop me from beginning. Now I am learning that I do not need to feel ready first. I can simply show up and try, and allow my strength and confidence to follow.

Fitness has given me more than physical change. It has shown me that I can care for my body and gradually reshape my life.

#### I Moved Closer to People and the Stage

I attended television recordings as an audience member and even tried being a contestant. I took painting lessons, completed a two-month improvisation course, and practised starting conversations with strangers.

I also began going alone to different comedy clubs to watch people tell their stories onstage. Eventually, I enrolled in a two-month stand-up comedy course of my own.

I met new people, and my social circle slowly grew.

I used to think the stage belonged only to people who were naturally outgoing and funny. Now I understand that courage does not mean feeling no fear. It means taking one more step while still feeling nervous.

#### I Still Don't Have the Answer to Dating

This year, I tried speed dating, slow dating, and online dating.

I genuinely hoped to find a long-term relationship and ended up disappointed. Even now, I am not completely certain what kind of relationship I want.

The experience did teach me something about myself: I can admire another person's freedom, independence, or ability to enjoy his own company, then build a filter around him because I like what those qualities represent.

Instead of worshipping the life someone else appears to have, I can build those qualities into my own life.

#### I Can Become Someone I Think Is Cool

I thought people who rode motorcycles looked cool, so I signed up for motorcycle lessons.

Once I began learning, that distant sense of admiration became smaller. Many things that look impressive are not reserved for a special kind of person.

I can learn them too.

I can become someone I think is cool.

#### My First Solo Trip to Bali

This year, I finally travelled to Bali by myself.

Before leaving, I imagined dating and meeting handsome men. Once I arrived, I tried many completely different things instead: paragliding with strangers, going to a bar alone, working out, riding motorcycles, sitting beside the ocean, and trying to make new friends.

In the end, I did not make many lasting friendships.

But I discovered that a journey does not need to produce a crowd of new friends to be successful. Returning to my room alone, eating alone, and choosing my next destination alone could also feel peaceful.

I did not find a relationship in Bali. Instead, I returned loving my own life more and appreciating everything I already had.

#### The World Suddenly Became Bigger

After I returned from Bali, my company offered me an opportunity to study in the United States.

Suddenly, the world felt enormous and exciting.

For years, I seemed to stand in the same place, believing life consisted only of work, relationships, and the worries directly in front of me. Once I stepped outside, I found gyms, stages, painting, motorcycles, travel, strangers, and so many places I had not yet reached.

#### Staying Soft After Seeing the World

While travelling, I met people who had already visited many places and experienced many extraordinary things.

After seeing so much of the world, some of them no longer seemed to admire romantic love, and very little could genuinely excite them. Perhaps when novelty and stimulation become ordinary, it becomes harder to feel moved.

I do not want that to happen to me.

No matter how many countries I visit or how much of the world I eventually see, I hope I keep a soft heart. I want to remain capable of love and kindness—and still feel happy because of a sunset, an ocean breeze, a delicious meal, or a small act of generosity from a stranger.

Being worldly should not mean becoming indifferent. Perhaps rich experiences should not teach us to say, “Is that all?” They should help us understand different lives and appreciate our own more deeply.

I want to keep exploring outward without losing my ability to feel joy within.

After seeing the world, I still want to be grateful for it.

Leaving my comfort zone did not magically make my life perfect.

I still feel lonely. Relationships still hurt me. I still become afraid and uncertain about where to go next.

But now I know that even when nobody comes with me, I can still leave.

A life cannot be measured only in years. Sometimes one year lived deliberately can leave more memories than seven years spent repeating the same routine.

In 2026, I did not find every answer.

But I finally began living again.
