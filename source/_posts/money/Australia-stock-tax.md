### Why Compounding and Time Matter in Investing
- Accumulating the first $100,000 takes the longest. Keep investing regularly and let the snowball grow. Only invest money that you will not need for a long time.
- `People Tend To Overestimate What Can Be Done In One Year`
- `And Underestimate What Can Be Done In Five Or Ten Years`
- Hold for the long term, trade less often, do not chase rallies or panic-sell, and invest regularly.

### Basic Concepts
1. Capital gain: the profit made when an asset is sold.
2. Dividend: a distribution paid by a company to shareholders.
3. Distribution: a payment made by a fund to investors.
   - After a dividend or distribution is paid, the share or unit price will generally fall by approximately the amount paid, all else being equal.
4. Dividend reinvestment plans (DRPs) still have tax-reporting implications.
   - You cannot claim an immediate deduction for some costs related to purchasing shares, such as brokerage fees and stamp duty, but you can include them in the cost base—the ownership cost deducted from the proceeds when calculating a capital gain or loss.
   - Cash dividends are normally the default. To join a DRP, update the election through the share registry—for example, CBA uses Link Market Services and VAS uses Computershare—not through the brokerage platform.
5. The S&P/ASX 200 is Australia’s benchmark share-market index.
6. CHESS sponsorship:
   - Share ownership records are stored and managed electronically. A Holder Identification Number (HIN) links an investor to their holdings and transaction records. CHESS provides secure and efficient share trading, clearing, and settlement in the Australian market.
   - With a CHESS-sponsored broker, the holdings remain registered to the investor even if the trading platform fails.
   - Examples of ETF issuers and funds include BlackRock/iShares (IOZ), State Street/SPDR, BetaShares (A200), and Vanguard (VAS).

### Capital Gains Tax (CGT)
- CGT applies to a capital gain realised when an investment is sold. The net capital gain is included in assessable income and taxed at the investor’s marginal rate.
- Australian individuals are generally eligible for a 50% CGT discount when they have owned an asset for at least 12 months, encouraging long-term investment.

### Capital Losses
- You cannot deduct a net capital loss from ordinary income, but you can carry it forward and apply it against capital gains in later years. There is no time limit for carrying forward a net capital loss.
- This applies when shares, ETFs, or other investments are sold at a loss.
- A capital loss can offset capital gains from other investments, but not salary or other ordinary income.
- For example, if ETF A has a $5,000 capital gain and ETF B has a $15,000 capital loss, the net capital loss is $10,000. It can be carried forward to a later financial year but cannot be deducted from ordinary income.

### Returns from Shares
1. Capital appreciation: the difference between the purchase and sale prices.
2. Dividends.

### Bonds
- Bonds are fixed-income investments through which governments or companies borrow money.
- Features:
  - Relative safety.
  - Priority of repayment over shareholders.
  - Stable returns through fixed interest payments.
  - Tradability on a secondary market.
- Australian market:
  - Government bonds: https://www.australiangovernmentbonds.gov.au/ — individual issues may have low trading volume and liquidity.
  - Corporate bonds: https://xtbs.com.au/ — individual issues may also have low trading volume and liquidity.
  - Bond ETFs combine multiple bonds, providing diversification, better liquidity, and easier trading.
    - Vanguard Australian Fixed Interest Index ETF (VAF): quarterly distributions; management fee noted as 0.2%.
    - iShares Core Composite Bond ETF (IAF): management fee noted as 0.15%.
    - BetaShares Australian Bank Senior Floating Rate Bond ETF (QPON): management fee noted as 0.22%; monthly distributions.
    - The source notes also state that financials and materials make up 40% of the ASX 200; this appears to be a separate observation rather than a characteristic of QPON.
    - Falling interest rates can reduce income from floating-rate bonds, while rising rates can make them more attractive.

### 一些基本概念
1. 资本利得（Capital gain）：卖出资产时获得的利润。
2. 股息（Dividend）：公司向股东支付的分红。
3. 基金分配（Distribution）：基金向投资者支付的收益。
   - 在其他条件不变时，支付股息或基金分配后，股票或基金单位价格通常会下降约等于支付金额的幅度。
4. 股息再投资计划（Dividend Reinvestment Plan，DRP）仍然涉及税务申报。
   - 购买股票时产生的某些费用（如经纪费和印花税）不能立即作为税务抵扣，但可以计入成本基础。计算资本利得或资本损失时，从处置资产所得中扣除该持有成本。
   - 默认通常是现金分红。如需加入 DRP，应通过股票登记机构修改，而不是通过股票交易平台。例如，CBA 使用 Link Market Services，VAS 使用 Computershare。
5. S&P/ASX 200 是澳大利亚基准股票市场指数。
6. CHESS 赞助：
   - 股票所有权记录以电子形式存储和管理。持有人识别号码（HIN）将投资者与其持仓及交易记录关联起来。CHESS 为澳大利亚市场提供安全、高效的股票交易、清算和结算。
   - 使用 CHESS 赞助的经纪商时，即使交易平台倒闭，持仓仍登记在投资者名下。
   - ETF 发行商及基金示例包括 BlackRock/iShares（IOZ）、State Street/SPDR、BetaShares（A200）和 Vanguard（VAS）。

### 资本利得税（CGT）
- 投资升值并卖出后实现的资本利得需要纳税。净资本利得会计入应税收入，并按投资者的边际税率征税。
- 澳大利亚个人持有资产至少 12 个月后再出售，通常可以享受 50% 的 CGT 折扣，这有利于长期投资。

### 资本损失
- 净资本损失不能从普通收入中扣除，但可以结转，并在以后年度抵扣资本利得。净资本损失的结转没有时间限制。
- 这适用于亏损卖出的股票、ETF 或其他投资。
- 资本损失只能抵扣其他投资的资本利得，不能抵扣工资或其他普通收入。
- 例如，ETF A 获得 5,000 澳元资本利得，ETF B 产生 15,000 澳元资本损失，则净资本损失为 10,000 澳元。这笔损失可以结转到后续财年，但不能从普通收入中扣除。

### 股票收益
1. 资本增值：买入价与卖出价之间的差额。
2. 股票分红。

### 债券
- 债券属于固定收益类投资，政府或企业通过发行债券借款。
- 特点：
  - 相对安全。
  - 偿还优先级高于股东。
  - 通过固定利息提供相对稳定的收益。
  - 可以在二级市场交易。
- 澳大利亚市场：
  - 政府债券：https://www.australiangovernmentbonds.gov.au/ —— 个别债券的成交量和流动性可能较低。
  - 企业债券：https://xtbs.com.au/ —— 个别债券的成交量和流动性也可能较低。
  - 债券 ETF 由多只债券组成，可以分散风险，流动性较好，也更容易买卖。
    - Vanguard Australian Fixed Interest Index ETF（VAF）：每季度分配收益；记录的管理费为 0.2%。
    - iShares Core Composite Bond ETF（IAF）：记录的管理费为 0.15%。
    - BetaShares Australian Bank Senior Floating Rate Bond ETF（QPON）：记录的管理费为 0.22%；每月分配收益。
    - 原笔记还写到金融和材料行业占 ASX 200 的 40%；这似乎是一条独立观察，而不是 QPON 的特征。
    - 降息可能降低浮息债券的收入，而加息时这类债券可能更有吸引力。
